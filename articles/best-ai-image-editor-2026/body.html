How we tested (methodology)

We tested each tool against the same five real-world editing tasks:

  1. Remove a person from a wedding photo — using Generative Remove / Inpaint tools. We graded on whether the removed region blended naturally with the surrounding scene.
  2. Replace a flat sky with a sunset — using Generative Replace / Sky Replace. We graded on lighting consistency and edge quality.
  3. Expand a portrait from square to 16:9 — using Generative Expand / Outpaint. We graded on identity preservation of the subject and how natural the extended scene looked.
  4. Swap a product background for e-commerce — using Background Replace. We graded on edge quality around hair/cloth and color matching.
  5. Apply a consistent style across a 10-image product set — using Style Reference / Brand Kit. We graded on consistency and whether the style survived across all 10 images.

We graded on:

  • Output quality — does the result look professional, or does it have obvious AI artifacts?
  • Identity preservation — does the human subject still look like themselves?
  • Manual control — can I refine the AI output with traditional tools (masks, brushes, layers)?
  • Batch support — can I apply the same edit to 10+ images without manually repeating each step?
  • Export — what formats can I export, and at what quality?

The AI image editors we recommend

⭐ Top Pick

1. Adobe Photoshop with Generative Fill — best for professionals

$20/mo Photography / $60/mo Creative Cloud

Photoshop is the only tool we tested that combines precise manual control (layers, masks, Smart Objects, channels) with high-quality generative editing (Firefly-powered Generative Fill, Expand, Remove, Replace). For professional photo and design work, this combination is the entire point — you can let the AI do the heavy lifting and then refine the result with the manual tools no other editor has.

The 2026 release (Photoshop v28) added the Firefly 3 model under the hood, which significantly improved identity preservation, skin texture, and lighting consistency. We expanded a portrait from square to 16:9 in the test, and the extended region looked like it was always part of the photo — the original lighting, skin tone, and even the hairline continued naturally. That was a noticeable step up from the 2025 release.

The Reference Image feature lets you upload a second image as a style reference, and the AI matches the lighting, color grade, and overall mood. We tested this on a product set — uploaded one hero image as the reference, and the AI applied the same lighting to the other nine products. Useful for e-commerce and brand work.

Where it falls short: subscription only — no perpetual license. The Photography plan at $20/mo doesn't include Generative Fill in older PSD workflows; you need the full Creative Cloud or the standalone Photoshop plan at $23/mo for the generative features. Adobe's pricing has gotten complicated, and the cheapest plan that unlocks everything is the Creative Cloud All Apps at $60/mo.

Pricing verified August 2026. adobe.com/photoshop

🥈 Runner-up

2. Recraft — best for designers (vector + raster + brand kits)

Free / $12/mo Pro / $48/mo Business

Recraft is the strongest standalone AI image editor we tested, and it is the only one with vector output (SVG). That alone makes it the right tool for designers who need to drop an AI edit into Figma or Illustrator and have it scale. The Pro plan at $12/mo includes unlimited generations, brand kits for style consistency, and the full edit toolkit.

The brand style kits are the killer feature. You upload 5-10 brand images, define the style once, and every subsequent generation and edit pulls from that style. We tested it on a 10-image product set and got consistent color grading, lighting, and typography across all 10 — the kind of consistency you usually only get from a real brand shoot.

Where it falls short: less suited for photographers than for designers. Recraft is built around the design workflow (icons, illustrations, posters, product shots). For wedding photos and portrait retouching, Photoshop or Lightroom is still better.

Source: recraft.ai

💰 Best Value

3. Clipdrop (by Jasper) — best for quick browser edits

Free / $11/mo Pro / $24/mo Team

Clipdrop is the tool to reach for when you need to remove a background, clean up an image, or uncrop a photo without installing anything. It runs in the browser, and the free tier gives you 100 image edits per day with no watermark. The Pro plan at $11/mo removes the daily cap and unlocks higher resolution.

It uses Stable Diffusion XL under the hood, which means the in-painting quality is genuinely good — we tested it on the wedding-photo remove-a-person task and the result was natural enough to be wedding-photographer-portfolio-quality. The Uncrop feature (extend the canvas) is the most-used tool in our testing because it handles awkward aspect ratios without needing Photoshop.

Where it falls short: no manual control — what the AI generates is what you get. For fine-tuning, you'd still need Photoshop. Clipdrop is the right tool for "I just need this one thing done," not for "I'm working on a magazine layout."

Source: clipdrop.co

4. Adobe Lightroom with Generative Remove — best for photographers

$10/mo Lightroom

Lightroom is the right AI image editor for photographers who don't need Photoshop's full power. The $10/mo plan gets you Generative Remove (the AI-powered spot healing), Generative Expand, and the masking AI that can select subject, sky, and background automatically. The mobile app has the same AI features, which is genuinely useful for editing on the road.

The batch style transfer is the most underrated Lightroom feature. You edit one photo in a set, apply the edit as a preset, and the AI applies it to the rest of the set with content-aware adjustments (each photo gets a slightly different crop or exposure to match the preset). For wedding and event photographers processing 500+ images a day, this is a real productivity gain.

Where it falls short: no generative fill — you can remove things, but you can't add things. If your workflow involves compositing (adding a new sky, swapping a background), you need Photoshop.

Source: Adobe Lightroom

5. Midjourney v7 Edit — best for editorial and stylized editing

$10/mo Basic / $30/mo Standard / $60/mo Pro

Midjourney v7 Edit is the tool to reach for when the goal is artistic re-interpretation, not photorealistic editing. We asked v7 to "make this portrait look like a 1970s film still" and it delivered — the result was a stylized image with film grain, muted colors, and period-accurate lighting. No other tool on this list can match that aesthetic.

The v7 Edit mode improved significantly over v6 — in-painting now respects the surrounding lighting and texture, and you can specify style references across a session so multiple edits feel cohesive. For editorial, fashion, and music industry work, Midjourney v7 is genuinely the best tool.

Where it falls short: not for product/photorealistic edits. The AI re-interprets aggressively, which is the point for editorial work and a bug for product photography. Midjourney's commercial rights are also tied to the $30/mo Standard plan and above; the $10/mo Basic plan is for personal use only.

Source: midjourney.com

6. Runway Gen-4 Edit — best when you also need AI video

$15/mo Standard / $35/mo Pro / $95/mo Unlimited

Runway Gen-4 Edit is the right AI image editor if you also need AI video editing in the same subscription. The Pro plan at $35/mo unlocks Gen-4 image editing (in-painting, replacement, style transfer) and Gen-4 video (motion brush, camera control, text-to-video). For creators doing both image and video, one subscription covers both.

The collaborative canvas is genuinely good for teams — multiple people can edit the same image at once, with version history. We tested this on a marketing team workflow (designer + copywriter + art director on the same image) and the canvas handled it without conflicts.

Where it falls short: expensive if you only edit images. The $15/mo Standard plan gives you Gen-3 video but limits image edits; the $35/mo Pro plan is the first one with full Gen-4 image editing. If you don't need video, Clipdrop or Recraft are cheaper for the same image editing quality.

Source: runwayml.com

⏭️ Skip

Skip: Google Imagen Editor — developer-only

Free in AI Studio / paid via Vertex AI

Imagen Editor is API-first with no consumer UI. If you're a developer integrating image editing into your app, it's cheap at scale (~$0.02 per image edit). If you need a UI to actually use the tool, every other option on this list is better.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI image editor in 2026?

Adobe Photoshop with Generative Fill is the best tool for professional photo and design work — no other tool combines its manual precision with high-quality generative editing. For designers who don't want Adobe's subscription, Recraft is the strongest standalone option, especially because it can output vector (SVG). For quick browser-based edits, Clipdrop Pro at $11/mo is the best value.

Are free AI image editors worth using?

Yes, for occasional edits. Clipdrop gives you 100 free image edits per day with no watermark, which is enough for one-off work. Recraft's free tier includes vector output. Midjourney v7 has a limited free trial. Adobe Firefly is free in Adobe Express if you can live with the watermark.

How do you test AI image editors?

We tested each tool against the same five tasks: remove a person from a wedding photo, replace a flat sky with a sunset, expand a portrait from square to 16:9, swap a product background for e-commerce, and apply a consistent style across a 10-image product set. We graded on output quality, identity preservation, batch support, and export format options.

Which AI image editor preserves identity best?

Adobe Photoshop Generative Fill and Lightroom Generative Remove preserve identity best in our tests. They are explicitly designed for photo editing where the human subject must look like themselves. Midjourney v7 Edit and Runway Gen-4 are stronger at re-interpretation but risk changing the subject's appearance.

Can AI image editors handle batch work?

Some can. Clipdrop has an API for batch. Lightroom's batch editing + style transfer is the best for photographers processing wedding/event sets. Photoshop requires manual batch via actions. Midjourney and Runway do not natively batch but allow per-image regeneration.

What about AI video editors?

If you also need AI video editing, Runway Gen-4 is the only tool on this list that covers both — image and video in one subscription. Otherwise, see our separate roundup of best AI video editors.
