Which detector should you pick

  • If you teach or grade in a school or university - GPTZero. Free tier covers 10,000 words/mo (enough for individual classrooms), Canvas integration removes the upload friction, and the perplexity scores help explain the verdict to students.

Pricing and plan notes that matter

AI detector pricing in 2026 has two structural shapes: credit-based (Originality.AI, Winston AI, Copyleaks) and word-based subscriptions (GPTZero, Sapling). Credit-based tools charge per check and reward bulk purchases - good for variable volume, punishing if you don't track your monthly volume. Word-based subscriptions are predictable but lock you into monthly minimums regardless of use.

Three traps to watch. First, Originality.AI's Pro plan credits expire monthly - unused credits don't roll over. If you have a slow month, you lose the value. Second, Copyleaks Pro at $74.99/mo annual is the most expensive tier in this roundup; the Personal tier at $13.99/mo annual covers 1,200 credits which is fine for individual publishers but not for teams. Third, Winston AI's credit math is the most confusing - AI detection uses 1 credit per word, plagiarism uses 2 credits per word, and AI image detection uses 200-500 credits per image. A 1,500-word AI scan uses 1,500 credits, so the Essential tier ($10/mo annual for 80,000 credits) only covers ~53 AI scans per month.

Free vs paid: when to upgrade

The free tiers in this roundup cover different jobs. Sapling's free unlimited detector is the most generous but the lowest accuracy. GPTZero's 10,000 words/mo free tier is the best fit for individual teachers and small publishers. Copyleaks' 10 pages/mo free tier is the lowest-volume paid-tier option. Originality.AI has only a trial, not a permanent free plan. The three thresholds that justify upgrading from free to paid:

  • Volume threshold - once you exceed 10,000 words/mo (GPTZero free), 10 pages/mo (Copyleaks free), or 5,000 words/mo (Sapling accuracy floor), paid tiers pay for themselves
  • Accuracy threshold - when false positives carry real consequences (academic misconduct, content takedown), 90%+ accuracy becomes non-negotiable
  • Compliance threshold - LMS integration, audit logs, and enterprise SLAs are paid-tier-only features

Below those thresholds, free tiers cover the job. Above them, paid tiers are worth the cost.

Edited content is the real accuracy story

The single largest accuracy drop across all detectors in 2026 is on edited AI content. Originality.AI drops from 94% to 71% on lightly edited AI text. GPTZero drops from 88% to 58%. Sapling drops from 79% to 48%. If your workflow involves paraphrasing or human-edited AI drafts, run multiple detectors, look at edit history, and pair detector scores with human review. No single detector score should be the basis for an academic misconduct decision or a content takedown without supporting evidence.

If you publish at scale and need the strongest accuracy + plagiarism in one credit, Originality.AI is the best fit. For education, start with GPTZero's free tier.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most accurate AI content detector in 2026?

Among paid tools, Originality.AI is the most accurate (94% on unedited AI, 97% on human-written) for publisher and content-agency workflows. Winston AI has the best accuracy on short content under 500 words (91%) and bundles OCR + plagiarism. GPTZero leads in education (88% unedited AI) thanks to its Canvas LMS integration and Turnitin partnership. All detectors degrade on edited AI content - pair any detector score with edit history and human review for high-stakes decisions.

Is there a free AI detector that is actually reliable?

Sapling's free detector is the most accessible (no account required, unlimited words), but accuracy is lower than paid tools - particularly on edited content. GPTZero's free plan (10,000 words/mo) offers a good middle ground for individual educators. For high-stakes decisions, a paid tool is worth the cost. Even the best paid detectors lose 10-30 percentage points of accuracy on lightly edited AI text.

Do AI detectors work on edited or paraphrased content?

All AI detectors degrade significantly on edited content. Originality.AI drops from 94% to 71% accuracy on lightly edited AI. GPTZero drops from 88% to 58%. Sapling drops from 79% to 48%. If you are evaluating content that has been paraphrased, run multiple detectors, look at edit history, and pair the detector score with human review. No single detector score should be the basis for an academic misconduct decision or a content takedown.

Which AI detector is best for education?

GPTZero. It was built specifically for educators and has become the most widely used AI detector in academic settings. The free tier (10,000 words/mo) is generous for individual teachers, the Essential tier ($10/mo) covers most classroom needs, and the Canvas LMS integration plus Turnitin partnership make it the easiest fit for K-12 and higher-ed. Perplexity scores and writing reports help explain the verdict to students rather than just returning a number.

Can I detect AI-generated code?

Yes - Copyleaks now includes source code AI detection alongside text detection. This is the right tool if you are reviewing student code submissions or evaluating contractor deliverables. Originality.AI does not currently advertise code detection, and the other detectors in this roundup focus on text only. Verify directly with Copyleaks before committing - this is a fast-moving feature category.
