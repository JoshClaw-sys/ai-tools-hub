Quick verdict — our top pick

AI mental health apps in 2026 fall into four categories: clinically-validated CBT chatbots (Wysa, Woebot), mood-tracking companions (Earkick, Mindstrong), general AI companions that include mental health features (Replika), and therapist-augmentation tools (Lyssn). Picking the right one depends on what kind of support you want and whether you need human escalation paths.

Wysa remains the most clinically-validated AI mental health app in 2026 — CBT-based chatbot, mood tracking, and human-coach escalation paths in one app. For users who want a warmer AI-only experience without the clinical framing, Earkick and Woebot are the strongest standalone picks.

Best for most people: the top pick below.
Best for budget: one of the free or low-cost options in the comparison.
Best for teams / enterprise: a tier with admin controls, SSO, and audit logs.

Key takeaways:

  • Wysa has the strongest clinical evidence base (multiple RCTs) and human-coach escalation.
  • Woebot is the simplest, fastest CBT chat experience — best for users who want low-friction daily check-ins.
  • Earkick is the best consumer-grade AI mood tracker with multi-modal input (voice, photo, text).
  • Youper, Replika, and Mindstrong round out the category with deeper personalization.

Who should buy what

  • Between therapy sessions — Wysa or Woebot for CBT-based daily check-ins that complement work with a real therapist.
  • On a therapy waitlist — Wysa (with optional human coach) or Earkick for structured support while you wait.

  • Workplace / benefits — Wysa for Employers, Lyra, or Spring Health — all have enterprise plans with human therapist escalation.

  • Anyone in crisis — these apps are NOT crisis services. In the US, call or text 988. Find your local crisis line at findahelpline.com.

Pricing and plan notes that matter

Most productivity tools have moved to per-seat SaaS pricing in 2026 — even when the underlying technology (a single model API call) is roughly the same cost across vendors. The differentiator is now workflow features: call recording, CRM sync, analytics dashboards, and integration depth. A cheaper tool that doesn't pipe into Salesforce is often more expensive in rep time than a pricier tool that does.

Free tiers are worth testing for a 14-30 day trial before committing. Most vendors let you upgrade mid-cycle and prorate the difference.

Free vs paid: when to upgrade

Free tiers in 2026 cover the core feature in almost every category we tested. The paid upgrade is justified when you hit one of: (1) usage caps on the free plan, (2) need for team/admin features, (3) need for compliance certifications (SOC 2, HIPAA), or (4) integration depth that the free tier doesn't expose.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI therapy app in 2026?

Wysa is the most clinically validated pick — multiple RCTs, CBT methodology, and human-coach escalation. Woebot is the simplest standalone CBT chat; Earkick is the best mood-tracking companion with multi-modal input.

Are AI therapy apps safe?

The apps on this list (Wysa, Woebot, Earkick, Youper) have transparent privacy practices and either clinical validation or published methodology. They are NOT a substitute for crisis care or licensed therapy for serious mental health conditions — if you are in crisis, contact 988 (US) or your local emergency service.

How much do AI mental health apps cost?

Most have a free tier (basic mood tracking + limited chat); paid tiers range from $7-30/month. Wysa and Woebot enterprise plans are priced per-employee for workplace benefits.

Can AI therapy apps replace a therapist?

No. These apps are best for between-session support, self-coaching, mood tracking, and skill building (CBT, mindfulness). For diagnosis, trauma, or serious conditions, see a licensed therapist. Some apps (Wysa, Lyra) offer hybrid AI + human-coach or AI + therapist pathways.

Do AI therapy apps keep my data private?

The apps on this list publish HIPAA-grade or equivalent privacy practices. Read the privacy policy before sharing anything sensitive — avoid AI mental health apps without a clear data policy or that sell data to third parties.
