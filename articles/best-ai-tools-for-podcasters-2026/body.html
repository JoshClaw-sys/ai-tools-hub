Pitfalls to avoid when adopting AI podcast tools

The most common mistake in 2026 is over-trusting AI filler-word removal. Descript's filler-removal pass is genuinely good, but it occasionally cuts a word that was intentional - a dramatic pause, a rhetorical "and," or a phrase the host uses as a signature. Always review the auto-edit pass before exporting the final cut, especially on interview episodes where the guest's cadence matters.

A second pitfall is Studio Sound overuse. Descript's Studio Sound cleans up remote audio beautifully, but pushing it to maximum intensity can introduce an unnatural "compressed" quality on voices that were already clean. Run Studio Sound at moderate intensity on guest tracks and reserve the highest setting for tracks that genuinely need it (background hum, room echo, fan noise).

Third, do not skip the transcription review before publishing. AI transcription in 2026 is at roughly 95-98% accuracy on clean studio audio, but that still leaves 5-15 errors per 10-minute episode on average - especially on brand names, technical terms, and proper nouns. A five-minute proofread pass before publishing catches the most embarrassing ones.

Finally, plan for storage costs. AI tools that transcribe locally (Descript, Riverside) generate large media files that can quickly fill a laptop's SSD. Budget for either an external drive or a paid cloud-storage tier if you are recording weekly episodes at high quality. The other hidden cost is transcription minutes - if you use Otter or Whisper in addition to Descript for backup transcripts, those minutes add up across a year of weekly episodes. Most working studios settle on a single transcription source to keep costs predictable.
