Pitfalls to avoid when picking an AI presentation maker

The biggest mistake teams make in 2026 is choosing a tool based on the demo screenshot alone. A tool that looks slick on a single template can fall apart once you ask it to produce a 20-slide deck with mixed content types (charts, tables, images, code blocks). Before committing to a paid plan, run the same real brief through at least three of the tools above - a 10-slide pitch deck with one chart, one table, and three bullet-heavy slides. Compare time-to-first-draft and the number of manual edits needed to reach a shareable state.

A second pitfall is ignoring export quality. PPTX export is not a solved problem in 2026 - layouts that look perfect in Gamma's web editor can lose alignment after import to PowerPoint, especially when custom fonts are involved. If your downstream workflow depends on PowerPoint edits, test the export with your real fonts and templates before you commit to a paid plan.

Third, watch for credit-gating on "unlimited" plans. Several tools in this category advertise unlimited generation but quietly throttle credit-heavy tasks (image generation, complex layouts, brand-kit updates) behind a separate quota. Read the fine print on the Pro tier's quota before you build a workflow that depends on it.

Finally, do not underestimate onboarding time. A team that has been using PowerPoint for five years will not switch to Gamma in a day. Plan at least two weeks of overlap where both tools are in use before cutting over the team's primary deck workflow. The teams that succeed with this kind of transition in 2026 all start with one low-stakes project - an internal update, a sales-enablement deck, a conference talk - rather than a high-stakes client deliverable.
