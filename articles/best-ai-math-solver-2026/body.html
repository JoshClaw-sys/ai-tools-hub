Who should buy which AI math solver

The right AI math solver depends on what kind of math you do and where you do it. Below is the decision logic that maps use cases to the right tool, plus the pricing traps we saw across all six tools in August 2026.

Pricing and plan notes that matter

  • Wolfram Alpha Pro is $7.25/mo or $5/mo for students - cheaper than Photomath Plus, and the Pro tier is the only way to unlock step-by-step on calculus and above in Wolfram.
  • Photomath Plus is $9.99/mo or $59.99/yr - the annual plan is a 50% discount and unlocks the AI tutor and textbook solutions. Without Plus, ads run on every problem.
  • Symbolab Pro is $9.99/mo or $5.99/mo annual - the annual plan is the cheapest paid step-by-step subscription in the roundup.
  • Mathway Plus is $9.99/mo or $39.99/yr - the cheapest annual option if you only need step-by-step on basic subjects.
  • Microsoft Math Solver is free - the only tool in this roundup with no paid tier at all.
  • Julius AI is $20/mo Pro or $40/user/mo Team - more expensive than dedicated math solvers but justified if math is one step in a larger data-analysis workflow.

Free vs paid: when to upgrade

  • If you only need basic high-school math - Microsoft Math Solver, Photomath free, or Wolfram's free tier will cover you. No upgrade needed.
  • If you need step-by-step on calculus or above - Wolfram Pro or Symbolab Pro. Both are under $10/mo and give the deepest explanations.
  • If you scan handwritten homework from a phone - Photomath Plus is the right upgrade. The free tier handles photo input, but Plus unlocks the AI tutor for follow-up questions.
  • If math sits inside a data workflow - Julius AI. The $20/mo Pro tier covers both math and data analysis, which is cheaper than paying for Wolfram plus a separate data tool.

Accuracy and what we trust

  • Wolfram Alpha uses symbolic computation, not pattern matching - when Wolfram says a derivative is right, it is right. This is the only tool in the roundup that publishes its math engine.
  • Symbolab and Photomath use a mix of symbolic math and AI-driven explanations - the step-by-step is reliable on standard textbook problems, less so on novel symbolic input.
  • Microsoft Math Solver and Mathway are pattern-matching solvers - they handle common textbook problems well and struggle on unusual phrasings.
  • Julius AI runs Python via GPT-4o and Claude - it is the most flexible for novel problems but its outputs depend on the underlying model's math reasoning, which can hallucinate.

The one we recommend skipping

Skip standalone AI chat tools like ChatGPT or Claude for math if accuracy matters. They can show plausible step-by-step work that turns out to be wrong, and they do not publish their math engine. For homework, use Wolfram or Symbolab. For a quick sanity check on a derivation, ask the chat tool to verify by writing the answer in Python and running it - but do not trust the chat output as the primary solution.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI math solver in 2026?

Wolfram Alpha is the strongest AI math solver for symbolic computation, calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. Photomath is the right pick for phone photo input, and Symbolab is the strongest free step-by-step option for algebra and calculus.

Can AI math solvers show step-by-step work?

Yes - Wolfram Alpha, Symbolab, Photomath, and Microsoft Math Solver all show step-by-step solutions on at least the basic subjects in their free tiers. Wolfram's step-by-step on advanced subjects requires Pro, and Photomath's deeper explanations require Plus.

How much does an AI math solver cost?

Most AI math solvers have a free tier that handles basic high-school math. Paid tiers run $5.99-$9.99/mo for step-by-step on more subjects and ad removal. Wolfram Pro is $7.25/mo or $5/mo for students.

Which AI math solver is best for calculus?

Wolfram Alpha is the strongest AI math solver for calculus - it handles symbolic differentiation, integration, series, and differential equations. Symbolab is the best free step-by-step option for calculus through multivariable and ODE basics.

Can AI math solvers read handwritten problems?

Yes - Photomath and Microsoft Math Solver both read handwritten or printed problems from a phone camera. Wolfram Alpha's mobile app also accepts photo input on iOS and Android.
