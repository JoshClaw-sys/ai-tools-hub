Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI tool for contract review in 2026?

Spellbook is our 2026 pick for most transactional lawyers — Word-native, transparent pricing, and the most-used contract-AI tool in the market. Harvey wins for biglaw willing to pay $600+/user/mo for a custom-trained legal LLM. Ironclad is the pick for in-house teams running CLM. The right choice depends on firm size and contract volume.

Are AI legal tools safe to use with confidential client data?

It depends on the tool's data handling posture. SOC 2 Type II compliance and a signable enterprise data processing agreement (DPA) are minimum requirements. Spellbook, Harvey, Ironclad, Lexis+ AI, and Westlaw Precision all meet these. Generic LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) should NOT be used for identifiable client contract review without an enterprise agreement that prohibits training on inputs — the hallucination risk is real.

Can AI replace a lawyer for contract review?

No, and it shouldn't. The strongest 2026 contract-AI tools position themselves as associate-level copilots: they draft, redline, and surface risks, but the lawyer remains responsible for substantive review and client advice. The current state of the art cuts first-pass contract review time by 50-70%, not 100%. Expect AI to multiply lawyer productivity, not replace lawyers.

How much do AI legal tools cost?

Solo and small-firm tools: Spellbook starts at $100-$200/user/mo with transparent pricing. Biglaw tools: Harvey is $600+/user/mo with custom enterprise contracts. In-house CLM platforms: Ironclad runs $40K-$200K+/year depending on contract volume. Legal research tools with AI (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw Precision AI) are typically bundled with existing Lexis/Westlaw subscriptions at incremental cost. Free trials are available from most vendors.

What features should I look for in an AI contract review tool?

Six things: (1) Word/DocX-native experience (lawyers live in Word — tools that require copying text into a separate UI lose 30% of the time savings), (2) hallucination resistance (does it cite real case law vs invent citations?), (3) redlining support (track changes, suggestion mode, not just comment generation), (4) playbook learning (can you teach it your firm's preferred clause library?), (5) SOC 2 + signable DPA for client-data handling, and (6) transparent pricing (no per-query metering that makes month-end unpredictable).
